M5 - the "works inside Revit" mark / 3 options

Replaces media/dm-button.png (a screenshot of the real ribbon button, now unused). Target use is M4: a small mark at the bottom of every suite window on bimsuite.html. All three are inline SVG, no raster, no external font files, no <defs> and no ids, so the same markup can be pasted many times on one page without id collisions.

Each is shown at 22 / 40 / 96 px height on the suite ground #f7f5fb and on the panel black #0b0913. No Autodesk or Revit logo or wordmark is used anywhere.

22PX IS THE REAL TEST

Option ARibbon Seat

Draws the context instead of the product: our orange tile sitting in a toolbar row between two grey ghost neighbours, with an active-tab underline beneath it, so the mark itself says "this is a button that lives in somebody else's ribbon" without naming or drawing anyone else's brand.

Weakness: the whole idea lives in the neighbours, and at small sizes the neighbours are the first thing to die - below about 34px it collapses into an orange smudge flanked by two grey nubs, which reads as dirt rather than as a toolbar.

22px: partial - shape survives, meaning does not

Option BFoam Tile

The SimLab move: one clean square app-tile and nothing else - the suite's 22px chamfer cut into a near-black tile, an orange oYmer Y, a cyan hairline, and an orange cut edge at the bottom-right corner. It is the only option whose entire content is one glyph, which is why it is the only one that cannot degrade.

Weakness: on its own it says "oYmer", not "Revit". The Revit half of the message has to come from the words next to it, so the tile is only half of a mark - it needs a caption in HTML, shown here to its right.

Option CSeat and Caption

Bakes the sentence into the SVG: the tile plus a two-line mono lockup, orange micro-label over a heavy line of ink, with textLength locking both runs so the box cannot be blown out by whatever mono the visitor happens to have. Self-contained - one element carries the whole message.

Weakness: it fails its own brief. At 22px tall the big line renders at about 7px and the micro-label at 4.5px; both are unreadable. It is also frozen English inside a Hebrew RTL page - not selectable, not translatable, and the word order cannot flip.

22px: fails - tile reads, type does not

Winner, tinted by the window it sits in (40px)

The tile reads --c for a faint wash over its fill, so it picks up the product hue already declared on .suite .c1-.c4 for free. The Y stays brand orange in all four - it is the oYmer mark, not a per-product accent. Custom properties only resolve from CSS, never from a presentation attribute, so every paint in these SVGs is written as a style="" declaration.

c1 DecisionMaker
c2 Viewer
c3 Family Creator
c4 Type Studio

Said plainly: at 13% over a near-black fill the tint is a whisper - the four tiles above are almost the same tile, which is the point (one mark, four windows). If you want the hue to actually read per product, change the hairline from rgba(2,242,255,.55) to rgb(var(--c)) and the tiles separate immediately - at the cost of losing the suite's cyan.

Why every option is dark-filled with a cyan hairline

  • Cyan rgba(2,242,255,.55) over #f7f5fb is almost invisible. On the light suite ground the hairline does nothing - the near-black fill is what carries the shape.
  • On #0b0913 the fill nearly matches the ground, so there the hairline carries the shape. The tile fill is lifted to #141024 rather than the panel's own #0b0913 so it does not sit flush against a dark panel.
  • That is the trade: each ground is served by a different part of the drawing, which is why the mark works on both without needing a light and a dark variant.

Trademark

  • No Autodesk or Revit logo, logotype or coloured mark appears in any option. Option A implies Revit purely by drawing a generic toolbar row.
  • Option C sets the plain word "REVIT" in our own mono. That is nominative text use, the same as the body copy already on the site, and is not their wordmark - but if you want zero exposure, the caption is HTML in the recommendation, so the word can be swapped or localised without touching the graphic.
