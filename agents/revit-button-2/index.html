The "works inside Revit" mark / attempt 2, four graphical options

Attempt one was three variations on a rounded square with a letter in it. This set throws that away. Every option here is a small picture with depth: isometric geometry, lit faces, a cast shadow, a wireframe half turning into a solid one, a bolt that lands somewhere. Still inline SVG, still no raster, no external font, no <defs>, no gradients and no ids at all, so the same markup can be pasted under all four suite windows on one page without colliding with itself.

Each is shown at 22 / 40 / 96 / 160 px on the suite ground #f7f5fb and on the panel black #0b0913. Same file on both grounds, no light and dark variant: the pale foam faces carry the shape on black, the dark outline and the cast shadow carry it on white. No Autodesk or Revit logo, logotype or colour appears anywhere.

Option AFoam Section

What it is: an isometric fragment of a building, exported as white foam, standing on a dashed ground plane with its own cast shadow. The whole right side has been sliced away and the cut face is filled solid orange with two floor levels ruled across it, which is the architect's own section poche convention; the exterior face keeps one dark window void so the block reads as a building rather than a box.

Weakness: a face that big in #ff7a00 is a lot of orange for a mark that sits four times on one page, and at a glance the cut can read as a warning colour rather than as a section.

22px: survives - the clearest of the four at true size, nothing thin to lose

Option BWall Strike

What it is: the suite's electric-bolt motif doing actual work. A wall panel with a window sits in the model as violet wireframe, a bolt comes down out of the top-right corner and lands on it, and the third of the wall it has already reached has turned into solid lit foam. The seam between wireframe and solid glows where the current is still touching it, so the mark shows a conversion in progress rather than a finished object.

Weakness: it is the busiest of the four and the wireframe grid is the first thing to die; it also needs its diagonal, so it is the only option that will not sit comfortably in a square 32px ribbon slot.

22px: partial - the bolt survives, the wall becomes a pale smear

Option CHalf Cube

What it is: one model cube caught mid-conversion. The far half is BIM wireframe, open, gridded and violet; the near half is white foam with lit and shaded faces; the vertical front edge where the two meet is a live orange bolt with a flash at the floor. It is the whole pipeline in one object, and its silhouette is a single hard hexagon, which is why it is the one that never turns into a smudge.

Weakness: the cube is generic geometry. On its own it says "a model became a foam model", not "Revit" - the Revit half of the sentence still has to come from the caption beside it. It is also the palest of the four, so on the light suite ground it leans entirely on its outline and its shadow.

22px: survives - the hexagon and the orange seam hold, but the wireframe half does not resolve as wireframe until about 30px

C as it would actually ship: the picture plus real HTML text

The caption is HTML, not baked into the SVG. It scales with the page type ramp, it flips correctly in the RTL suite pages, it is selectable and translatable, it can be dropped entirely below 560px, and it keeps the word out of the graphic file.

Option DSeated Inside

What it is: the most literal reading of the brief - a big hollow wireframe cage with cyan corner ticks is the host model, and our small solid foam block sits on its floor inside it, outlined in orange, with two orange rings spreading on the floor where it landed. It is the only one of the four that draws the word "inside" instead of implying it.

Weakness: the meaning lives entirely in the relationship between two objects, and the outer one is built from hairlines, so the moment the cage stops resolving the mark is just an orange nugget floating in nothing.

22px: fails - the cage dissolves and only the orange block is left

Why one file works on both grounds

  • The solid parts are white to pale lavender foam. On #0b0913 they glow on their own and the dark outline is invisible, which does not matter. On #f7f5fb the pale faces would vanish, so the #241b3c outline and the cast shadow are what hold the shape there.
  • The wireframe is #8b7fc0, a mid violet chosen because it clears both grounds. Behind it sits a wider cyan rgba(2,242,255,.45) halo stroke, which is near-invisible on white and becomes a neon rim on black. Each ground is served by a different layer of the same drawing.
  • #ff7a00 is the only paint that needs no help on either ground, so in every option it carries the single most important line: the cut, the strike, the seam, the outline of our block.

The 22px verdicts were measured, not guessed

  • Each mark was rendered at a true 22, 32 and 40 px, then that raster was magnified six times with nearest-neighbour so the actual pixels could be judged. The first pass of C failed that test on the light ground - a pale cube on a pale page. Its ink outline went 1.8 to 2.4, its violet outline 1.6 to 2.0, the wireframe fills roughly doubled, the bolt went 2.6 to 3.0 and the shadow from .14 to .18 opacity. Nothing about the drawing changed, only the weights, and it now holds at 22.
  • Ranked purely on legibility at a true 22px the order is A, C, B, D. A wins that contest because a large flat orange plane is the single most robust thing you can put in a small mark. That is also the reason it is not the recommendation - see NOTES.md.

Trademark

  • No Autodesk or Revit logo, logotype, glyph or brand colour is used. Nothing here is a picture of their UI either - attempt one's "Ribbon Seat" drew a generic toolbar; this set does not draw a toolbar at all.
  • The word "Revit" appears only as HTML body text in the ship example, which is ordinary nominative use and is trivially swappable to Hebrew without touching the graphic.

Traps carried over from attempt one

  • var() does not resolve inside an SVG presentation attribute. Every paint in this file is written as a style="" declaration for that reason. There are no custom properties in these SVGs at all, so nothing can silently render black.
  • No <defs>, no gradients, no filters, no ids. The 3D look is built from flat facets at three values, and the glow from stacked strokes, so the same markup can be duplicated freely on one page.
Recommendation: Option C, Half Cube, with Option A, Foam Section as the richer alternative anywhere it is guaranteed 96px or larger. See NOTES.md.
